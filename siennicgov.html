Sienna

About

Sienna is a micronation established by a group of pre-teens and teenagers who seized control of various neighborhoods, public structures, and local churches across the Tennessee region, including Kingsport, Bristol, and Johnson City. Over time, the nation has organized into a self-sustaining sovereign state with a formal administrative structure, distinct culture, independent economic market, and custom linguistic system.

1. Geography and Territorial Organization

The territorial expansion of Sienna originated with the barricading and takeover of the Ketron neighborhood in Bloomingdale, designated as Fortlund. Expansion rapidly followed with the acquisition of the White Top neighborhood in Bristol, designated as Towerlund, as well as several local churches throughout Johnson City and Kingsport.

The primary administrative seat and national capital operates directly out of a repurposed former elementary school. To accommodate citizens, suburban housing consists of customized, fully furnished residential sheds sourced from Lowe's and situated on an adjacent athletic football field. Public infrastructure includes a national hospital, a state airport, and primary recreational attractions such as the Infinity Pool located at the Pangea Resort. Regionally, the sovereign territories are categorized into four core states: Fortlund, Towerlund, Riverlund, and Peschi.

2. Culture, Society, and Demographics

Siennic society is predominantly governed and populated by youth, pre-teens, and teenagers. While youth direct the executive branches of state, adults participate in specialized religious or administrative roles, including serving as regional bishops.

Sienna operates under its own standardized temporal system known as Siennic Time Zone (STZ). Under STZ, time progresses at an accelerated rate where two standard real-world calendar weeks equal two official calendar months in Siennic time.

The nation maintains an active cultural and media output. Sienna manufactures custom CD productions, airs original regional television shows, and operates local broadcasting networks such as Fort City News 4. Domestic musical genres include S-pop and Regalhcs music.

3. Governance, Security, and Economy

Executive leadership is held by officials bearing the title of Redael, led by Chief Leader David Gold alongside designated Vice Redaels and State Redaels across individual territories. Public safety, law enforcement, and territorial defense fall under the authority of the Boarder Patrol. Dressed in tactical camouflage outfits, sunglasses, and tan hats, Boarder Patrol officers enforce entry protocols, require valid Siennic identification credentials, and conduct active patrols using custom Go-Karts and E-bikes.

The national legal tender is the Siennic Dollar, denoted by the character Ş. The state maintains an independent manufacturing industry producing local technological hardware, including custom personal computers and E-scooters, as well as specialized goods such as Ffuplabreh herbal cigarettes.

4. Language Family and Orthography

The official communication system consists of the Siennic language family, comprising three standardized regional variants: Siennic (spoken in Fortlund and Towerlund), Riverlundi (spoken in Riverlund), and Peschaten (spoken in Peschi). While English grammatical syntax is preserved, vocabulary undergoes systematic transformation across languages.

The primary script is the Nitalanneis alphabet, featuring 55 distinct characters including native Cyrillic-style symbols and custom orthographic markers. Shared linguistic features across all dialects include reciprocal W and M pronunciation rules (where W sounds as M, and M sounds as W), grammatical macron placement for uppercase letters, and the rolled consonant phoneme representation of double R (ʁ).

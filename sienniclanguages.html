1. Introduction

The Siennic languages are a small language family characterized by systematic reverse word formation while preserving English grammatical order. Rather than reversing sentence structure, Siennic languages transform individual lexical items according to language-specific rules.

Three standardized languages comprise the family:

  • Siennic
  • Riverlundi
  • Peschaten

Although closely related, each language employs a different method of transforming English vocabulary while maintaining nearly identical grammar.

2. The Siennic Language Family

The family consists of three official languages.

Language Official State
Siennic Fortlund, Towerlund
Riverlundi Riverlund
Peschaten Peschi

Siennic serves as the principal literary and administrative language of the family.

3. Geographic Distribution

The languages are spoken primarily in the Siennic states.

  • Fortlund — official language: Siennic
  • Towerlund — official language: Siennic
  • Riverlund — official language: Riverlundi
  • Peschi — official language: Peschaten

5. Common Characteristics

All Siennic languages share several defining linguistic features.

Word Order

All languages preserve English syntax. Only the internal structure of words changes.

Example:

English:

The man walks home.

The transformed sentence preserves the same grammatical order.

Letter Pronunciation

A unique phonological feature is the reciprocal pronunciation of W and M.

Written Pronounced As
W M
M W

The visual representation remains unchanged.

Uppercase Letters

Uppercase letters are considered grammatical markers rather than independent letters. Within the native alphabet they are represented by adding a macron to the previous translated character rather than by using separate uppercase glyphs.

Double R

Two consecutive R letters represent a separate phoneme.

Written Pronounced
rr ʁ
RR ʁ̄

This sound is a rolled consonant distinct from ordinary R.

6. Writing Systems

Two writing systems are recognized.

Nitalanneis Alphabet

The traditional script.

Latin Alphabet

The Latin alphabet is considerably more common in modern technology, computing, and international communication.

8. Siennic

Overview

Siennic transforms English by reversing every individual word while preserving:

  • sentence order,
  • punctuation,
  • capitalization positions,
  • grammatical structure.

Example

English:

Hello, how are you? Today is a great day, John.

Siennic:

Olleh, woh era uoy? Yadot si a taerg yad, Nhoj.

Pronunciation

Consonant clusters that become awkward after reversal are skimmed, meaning one or more consonants become silent to preserve fluent pronunciation.

Grammar

Grammar follows English almost entirely. No inversion of sentence order occurs.

Noun Cases

Siennic distinguishes three noun cases.

Proper Standard

Used when a sentence contains no proper nouns, or in some contexts when all nouns are treated generically.

Proper Noun

Original English proper nouns remain untranslated.

Example: Walmart remains Walmart. This case refers to preserving original nouns and should not be confused with the grammatical category of proper nouns in English.

Full Noun

All nouns are translated fully. Spaces within names are removed before transformation.

Example:

John Doe → JohnDoe → Eodnhoj

9. Riverlundi

Overview

Riverlundi transforms English according to reversed pronunciation clusters rather than ordinary spelling. Its grammar is identical to Siennic.

Letter Clusters

Riverlundi uses 76 pronunciation clusters. Words are converted into phonetic clusters before reversal.

Example:

Sunshine → Sun-shine → Sun-sh-ine → In-ihs-nuhs → Inihsnuhs

Single letters likewise become pronunciation clusters. Example: I becomes Eiy.

The pronunciation of clusters is literal, resembling Russian orthographic consistency.

Example

English:

You are looking very good today, sir.

Riverlundi:

Oy ehra guhnihkewl yivrev dewg yahdaht, rihs.

10. Peschaten

Overview

Peschaten reverses English words and then replaces the final reversed character according to the type of letter removed.

Replacement Rules

After reversing a word:

Removed Character Replacement
Vowel aten
Consonant itna
Semivowel vuhtvad
Single-letter word sht

Example

English:

Hello, John. I love you.

Peschaten:

Olleitna, Johitna. Isht evoaten ouaten.

11. Pronunciation Rules

All members of the family share the following pronunciation conventions.

  • W is always pronounced as M.
  • M is always pronounced as W.
  • Double R is pronounced as the distinct phoneme ʁ.
  • Riverlundi pronunciation follows literal pronunciation clusters.
  • Siennic permits silent consonants in difficult reversed clusters.

12. Special Characters

Three symbols are unique to Siennic orthography.

Character Purpose
Ş Siennic dollar sign
¿ Alternate question mark (entered using ? + 2)
ʁ Rolled double R

13. Summary

The Siennic language family comprises three closely related constructed languages sharing a common grammatical foundation while differing in lexical transformation methods.

  • Siennic reverses the spelling of each English word while preserving sentence order and grammatical structure.
  • Riverlundi reverses phonetic pronunciation clusters instead of written spelling.
  • Peschaten reverses spelling and then applies suffix replacement rules based on the final character type.

Across all three languages, shared features include the Nitalanneis writing system, reciprocal W/M pronunciation, grammatical uppercase marking via macrons, and the use of distinctive orthographic symbols such as Ş, ¿, and ʁ. Together, these characteristics define the Siennic language family as a cohesive linguistic system with consistent grammar but distinct methods of lexical transformation.
